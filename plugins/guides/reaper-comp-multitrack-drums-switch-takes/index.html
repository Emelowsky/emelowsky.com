How to comp multitrack drums in REAPER: switch the take on every mic at once

You recorded drums on eleven microphones, in three takes. Each mic is its own track, and each take is a take inside the item. Now you want the verse from take 2 and the chorus from take 3.

The problem: the take belongs to each item separately. Picking take 2 on the snare does not change the kick, the toms or the overheads - you have to do it on every mic. And if one cut ends up a few samples off on a single track, the mics are no longer in phase, which is often hard to hear until the drums suddenly sound thin.

Comping like playlists in Pro Tools

Individual Take Sync Group for REAPER links the items you recorded together into a group. From then on, every edit on one of them happens on all of them:

  • Switch the active take on one mic - every mic in the group switches to the same take
  • Split one track, and the whole group is split at the same point; the new parts join the group by themselves
  • Moves, trims and deletes follow too, sample accurate, so the phase between the mics stays intact
  • Several independent groups: drums, vocals, guitars

A typical comping pass

  1. Make a group from the drum tracks.
  2. Split at the start of the chorus on any mic - all mics are split.
  3. Pick take 3 for the chorus on the snare - all mics switch.
  4. Listen, move the cut a little if needed - the whole group follows.

Group order matters: keep the widest group (for example "All") at the top of the list, so edits flow from the biggest group down.
