How to mirror the pan of double-tracked guitars in REAPER

Double-tracked rhythm guitars are one of the most common things in a rock mix: the same part played twice, one take panned left, the other right. The width of that pair shapes the whole mix - and you usually try a few widths before you settle: hard left and right, 80%, 60%...

Every time you change it, you have to move two pan knobs and make sure they end up exactly mirrored. Get them a few percent apart and the guitars lean to one side, which you hear on headphones long before you see it on the screen.

One knob for both sides

Fast Group for REAPER has Reverse Pan: group the two guitars, turn on Reverse Pan, and the pans move as mirror images. Pan one guitar to 70% left and the other goes to 70% right - always symmetrical.

  • Reverse Pan per group - perfect for double-tracked guitars, doubled vocals or stereo keys recorded as two mono tracks
  • Volume stays linked too, with the offset between the two takes kept, even at the end of the fader
  • Mute, solo, record arm and polarity can follow the group
  • Pause the sync to fix one take, then resume
  • Save the setup as a group template and use it in every session

A tip: try the width with the whole mix playing, not solo. Pairs that sound great hard-panned on their own often sit better a little narrower once the bass and drums are in.
