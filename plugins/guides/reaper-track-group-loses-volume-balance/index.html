Why REAPER track groups lose your volume balance - and how to keep it

You set up a drum group: kick a bit louder, overheads lower, room mics way down. Everything sounds right. Then you push the group up for the chorus, one of the faders hits the top of its range, you pull the group back down - and the balance you built is gone. The tracks now sit closer together than before.

Why it happens

REAPER's native track grouping moves the members together, but each fader has its own limit. When one member reaches the end of its range it stops, while the others keep moving. On the way back they all move by the same amount again, so the difference that was "lost" at the limit never comes back. The same happens with pan at hard left or hard right.

In a quick session you may never notice. In a long mix with automation passes and many small rides, it slowly pushes your balance around - and you only hear it when the chorus suddenly feels flat.

What you can do in REAPER itself

  • Keep group faders away from the top of their range - leave headroom on every member.
  • Write down (or snapshot) the levels before big moves, so you can put them back.
  • Ride the group with a separate bus or folder fader instead of the grouped track faders.

All of these work, but they ask you to think about the fader instead of the music.

Keeping the offsets with Fast Group

Fast Group for REAPER is a grouping script that remembers the offset of every track in the group. Push the group to the top and back, and every track returns to exactly where it was relative to the others. The offsets are stored with the project, so they survive closing and reopening it.

  • Volume and pan offsets stay, also at the limits of the fader
  • One click (or Ctrl+G) makes a group from the selected tracks
  • Mute, solo, record arm and polarity can follow the group too
  • Group colours and badges on the tracks, group templates
